Export CSV — arms + settings

One table per block, newest first. Δ is measured against that block’s own baseline arm (shaded — the first control-looking arm, else the first arm). Deltas are computed within a block only: cross-block numbers in this project are not comparable, so they are never subtracted. acc is Qwen accepted/proposed. An arm with no [stats] line is marked partial — it was killed or crashed mid-run and its speed is not reported rather than being shown as zero. Memory columns are peak used / min available / peak GPU across the arm, never a mean — a mean over a window that includes the idle head and tail measures the sampler, not the engine. steady is decode with the first chunk excluded; tok/s fuses it with prefill and the cold spine load. That fixed cost is ~22.5% of a 40-token run and ~3% of a 200-token one, so the headline rate dilutes every delta, and dilutes short arms hardest — judge changes on steady. 1st tok is time to the first [stats] line: with drafting ON that line covers a whole chunk, so it reads as time-to-first-chunk rather than to one token. It is not fixed overhead — it moved 22.6 s→36.5 s (+59%) from expert placement alone. Click any arm row to expand the settings that arm actually resolved — overrides, I/O, residency, drafting, the engine’s own [native] declarations and its end-of-run counters. SSD draw act is the mean over samples above a 0.5 GB/s idle floor and duty is the share of samples above it — active-rate × duty, never mean-over-window: the same 846 GB of reads reads as 5.66 GB/s over 150 s and 8.95 GB/s over 95 s. Per-device columns use each arm’s own .map, because disk numbers change on every replug.

Export CSV — charged vs drawn

One row per arm that actually ran the scheduler, carrying both sides per drive: what the clocks charged (charged_*, counts and shares), what the drives did (drawn_gbs_*, drawn_pct_*, peak_gbs_*), the model constants being steered on (cost_us_*, cap_gbs_*), and the divergence gap_pp_* in percentage points — positive meaning a drive was charged more than it carried. That gap is the reason this tab exists: every expert record is the same 17,547,264 B, so charged share and drawn share should track. The full population says they do. The single arm that motivated this tab (K3C charged 23.5%, carrying 27.2%) does not generalise: across all 21 scheduler arms the divergence is a small fixed offset — internal −1.21 pp, K3C −0.86, K3B +1.30, K3A +0.77 — and it does not track speed, which it would if a constant were mispriced: correlation(tok_s, internal gap) = +0.056 across 0.3555–0.5827 tok/s. So this tab is now evidence the cost model is not the problem. The two arms with real divergence (R_ON, SCHED_ON, K3C ≈−4.4 pp) are the ones where the layout changed, and both were slower than their controls. Capability is suspect — but cap_peak cannot say which way. internal reports 12.72 GB/s against a rated 11.68. A device cannot exceed its own ceiling, so either the rating is understated or the metric is aliased. cap_peak is the max over 200 ms windows of byte deltas, bounded by device rate and duty cycle inside the window, so a drive whose bytes arrive in long contiguous bursts reports a higher “peak” than an equally fast drive with the same bytes spread thinner — and internal, carrying ~40% of traffic on one device, has the densest bursts. The symmetry is the point: if the metric is unreliable enough to show internal beating its ceiling, the enclosures’ −6 to −10% deficits are equally untrustworthy, and “the constants are miscalibrated against the fastest device” is not supported. Resolved by rerunning under k3-diskscope at 10 ms with rolling 100 ms maxima — precedent (2026-08-26) is that rolling-100 ms lands on capability while single-tick overshoots through completion batching. Rated constants stay unpatched until then. Arms where the scheduler was present but disabled emit an all-zero [mirror-split] and are excluded — there is no charge to compare. Unlike the table above this export is not capped at 30 arms; the cap keeps the page light, and silently truncating a download would misrepresent the population.

Live utilisation

Cost model the scheduler steers on

Each clock is charged RECORD / bandwidth per open and never drains, so ordering by it is weighted round-robin over lifetime cost, not least-loaded dispatch. Its fixed point is allocation proportional to bandwidth — which is optimal only if every drive is equally utilised at the margin.

Charged vs actually drawn

Δ is the diagnostic. charged is what the clocks believed they dispatched; drawn is what the drives actually moved. Every expert record is the same 17,547,264 B, so with equal duty these should track. Where they diverge, the cost constant for that drive is wrong — it is derived from sequential bandwidth, and K3C is an SN8100 with DRAM while K3A/K3B are DRAM-less SN7100s, so their behaviour under random 17.5 MB reads need not follow the sequential ratio. A drive charged less than it draws is being over-scheduled.
